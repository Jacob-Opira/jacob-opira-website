Buddhism — vipassanā and the two truths
Conventional truth (*saṃvṛti*) vs. ultimate truth (*paramārtha*). Ordinary sight sees persons, things, selves. *Insight* sees impermanence, unsatisfactoriness, not-self — a world hidden *by* the ordinary view, not behind it. Zen's *kenshō*: "seeing one's nature."

Plato — the cave and the two worlds
The physical world is shadow; the *noēton* (intelligible realm) is real. "Inner vision" here is *noēsis* — a kind of seeing with the mind that grasps Forms. The eye of the body sees copies; the eye of the soul sees originals. Plato's whole vocabulary of *illumination* (light, sun, clarity) becomes the template for later traditions.

Neoplatonism — the inward ascent
Plotinus: turn inward (*epistrophē*), and you find not a private self but a hierarchy — soul, intellect, the One. Inner vision is *upward* vision. The world hidden in plain sight is *your own ground*, which turns out to be everyone's ground.

Kabbalah — the four worlds
Emanation (*Atziluth*), Creation (*Beriah*), Formation (*Yetzirah*), Action (*Assiah*). The world we see is the outermost shell (*kelipah*); inner vision penetrates to the sparks (*nitzotzot*) hidden in every thing.

Advaita Vedanta — *drishti* and *darshan*
The world is *maya* — not "illusion" so much as *measurement*, the way the infinite appears when sliced by perception. *Darshan* is the seeing that *sees the seer*. The hidden world is not elsewhere; it is what remains when the seer stops mistaking themselves for the seen.

Phenomenology — the Lebenswelt
Husserl: beneath the world of science (*mathematical nature*) lies the *lifeworld* — the pre-scientific world of meaning, habit, and lived significance. We are blind to it because we live *in* it. Heidegger: the *clearing* (*Lichtung*) in which beings show up. The hidden thing is not another world but *the openness that lets any world appear*.
